# What stands between this and the App Store

Checked on 2026-09-07 against a Release archive that was exported, validated
and uploaded, the built binaries, both Info.plists, and the App Store Connect
record itself. Everything below was run rather than reasoned about; where
something could not be checked, it says so.

Version 1.0, build 2, uploaded and attached to the version. The app record is
`com.sachin.Motionary` under team `LP92N2QT42`.

## Blocks the submission

**The App Privacy questionnaire has to be answered in the browser.** It is the
one field the App Store Connect API does not expose, and the version cannot be
submitted until it is filled in. It is a single question - "do you or your
third-party partners collect data from this app" - and the answer is no, which
produces the "Data Not Collected" label.

## Very likely rejected

**About seventy recreated third-party brand icons ship in the bundle.** 29
recognisable marks across 369 distinct skins, in all three starters: Amazon,
App Store, Apple Music, ChatGPT, Chrome, Clash Royale, D&D Beyond, Discord,
Facebook, Gmail, Google, Google Maps, Instagram, Messenger, Microsoft Teams,
Netflix, PayPal, Reddit, Roblox, Snapchat, Spotify, TikTok, Uber, Uber Eats,
WhatsApp, X, YouTube, Zoom. Redrawing another company's mark and shipping it is
what icon-pack and theming apps are routinely rejected for.

**This is being shipped anyway, knowingly.** The decision was made on
2026-09-07 to submit and find out. What it costs if it goes wrong lands on the
Developer Program account the app publishes under, which is not the author's.

Worth being precise about what a fix would cost, because an earlier attempt got
this wrong: deleting the icon files does not remove the marks. They are baked
into `prebuilt-*-wallpaper.png`, and `Resources/prebuilt-*` is generated by the
install pipeline from the starter designs' own `Skins` folders. Shipping
without them means re-authoring the three starters with generic artwork and
re-baking their wallpapers, or shipping no starter designs at all. The app
already carries an Iconify-backed catalogue of open-source icons, so the
replacements do not have to be drawn from nothing.

## What it takes to produce an .ipa

Five separate things failed between "the code is fine" and "the store accepted
it". None of them were build errors, and none of them said what was wrong.

**The toolchain cannot be chosen by where an Apple ID is signed in.** The store
rejects anything built with a beta Xcode - build 1 was made with Xcode 27 beta
and could not be submitted. The only reason the beta was used is that it was
where the account was logged in, and `-allowProvisioningUpdates` needs an
authenticated account to register identifiers. `Tools/archive.sh` now takes
`ASC_KEY_ID` and `ASC_ISSUER_ID` and hands `xcodebuild` an App Store Connect
API key instead, so the GM Xcode can be used regardless. Check the result:
`DTXcode` in the built `Info.plist` is `2660` for the GM and `2700` for the
beta.

**A free personal team cannot carry App Groups.** Release archiving under one
resolves to the team's wildcard profile, which has no App Groups entitlement,
and stops with three errors that name the entitlement rather than the team.

**Bundle identifiers and app groups are globally unique.** `com.caden.*` is
registered to the old personal team and cannot be claimed by the new one, which
is why the identity moved to `com.sachin.*` wholesale. `group.com.sachin.Motionary`
was itself already taken, so the group is `group.com.sachin.motionary.shared` -
it does not have to match the bundle id, and here it cannot.

**`-allowProvisioningUpdates` is needed on the export as well as the archive.**
Automatic signing that can register an App ID but not a distribution profile
fails at the end with `No profiles ... Automatic signing is disabled`.

**A Homebrew rsync breaks the .ipa packaging step.** Xcode calls `rsync` by
name; 3.4.1 rejects the flags it passes and the export dies as `Copy failed`,
which names neither rsync nor the flag. The real error is only in
`IDEDistributionPipeline.log` inside the `.xcdistributionlogs` bundle. The
script now puts `/usr/bin` first for the export so Apple's own is used.

## Filling in the store record

Everything except App Privacy can be written through the App Store Connect API
with the same key the archive uses, which is worth knowing because the web form
silently loses work: two screenshots that appeared to have been uploaded were
not attached to the version at all.

- **Screenshots are 6.9" at 1320x2868**, produced by `Tools/store-shots.sh`.
  The API has no `APP_IPHONE_69`; 6.5", 6.7" and 6.9" all live in
  `APP_IPHONE_67`, and the web UI may offer a 6.5" slot that rejects them.
- **`whatsNew` cannot be set on a 1.0.** It only exists for updates, and the
  API returns a `STATE_ERROR` rather than saying so.
- **The age rating declaration wants all 22 attributes at once**, a mix of
  `NONE` enums and booleans. Every answer here is none or false, giving 4+.
- **Copyright is required** and is not part of any localisation: it is an
  attribute on the version.
- **The description is hard-wrapped in `app-store-listing.md`** for reading.
  The store renders newlines literally, so prose has to be rejoined before it
  is sent or the product page breaks mid-sentence.

## What passed

- **The archive builds, checks itself, exports, validates and uploads.**
  `Tools/archive.sh` verifies the four things App Store Connect rejects for
  silently and that a project regeneration can quietly drop: the asset catalog,
  the rendered icon, and both privacy manifests. Apple's own verify returned
  no errors and no warnings on build 2.
- **There is an app icon**, in three appearances - default, dark and tinted.
  Verified as rendered, not just as a file: `AppIcon60x60@2x.png` comes out of
  the built bundle correctly, and SpringBoard draws it on a clean install.
  Studio has one too, which it had been running without.
- **`PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy` is present in both targets.** `CA92.1` for
  `UserDefaults` in both; the app additionally declares `0A2A.1` for file
  timestamps in its own container. The extension's is deliberately shorter -
  declaring an API that is not used is its own kind of wrong answer.
- **`ITSAppUsesNonExemptEncryption` is set** in the built binary, not just in
  source, so uploads do not stop to ask the export-compliance question and no
  documentation has to be supplied.
- **`LSSupportsOpeningDocumentsInPlace` is declared false.** `CFBundleDocumentTypes`
  is declared for the delivery package, so Apple wants to be told how documents
  open; false is the accurate answer, because a package is copied into the
  app's Documents and unpacked rather than edited where it sits. Without it the
  upload carries warning 90737.
- **No private API in the shipping binaries.** The
  `_wantsCustomFontsEmbeddedInArchive` shim stays confined to
  `FONT_EMBED_PROBE`; `nm -u` finds it in neither binary.
- **Nothing forces an age rating above 4+.** There is no embedded browser - the
  only `WKWebView` in the tree is a comment in `SVGPathParser` describing an
  approach not taken - so the unrestricted-web-access question is a no even
  though the app opens links. `UIApplication.open` hands them to the system.
- **HealthKit and WeatherKit are gone**, along with the two readouts that could
  never have worked without entitlements the app does not carry. `otool -L`
  shows neither framework. The only location-adjacent thing left is the
  weak-linked Swift overlay EventKit brings in for the calendar readout, which
  is declared and used.
- **The build is no longer cut for one phone.** Three parts: the app and the
  extension read the screen at launch and derive geometry for it; the exported
  wallpaper is rescaled to that screen; and both renderers lay the composition
  out through `DeviceGeometry.pointsPerAuthoredPixel`, so a design cut on one
  canvas fills the screen of another rather than drawing at its authored width
  and leaving the rest black.

  The widget uses the screen ratio and has to. The wallpaper under it was
  exported at that ratio, and baking tiles into the wallpaper only works if a
  tile crossing the widget's edge reads whole - a widget drawn at any other
  scale cannot continue the picture it sits on. Dividing by the handed-over
  frame instead was tried and reverted: it assumes the placed widget is the
  family the design was cut for, and a `systemLarge` standing in for the
  portrait extra-large then shrank the whole composition by 2.8%.

  Swept across every iPhone simulator size with `Tools/geometry-sweep.sh`.
  **The composition reaches both edges on all five**, where a design cut for a
  narrower canvas used to stop short and leave black:

  | device | screen | drawn | widget handed over | dx | dy |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | iPhone 17e | 1170x2532 | 1170/1170 | 342x358pt | +10 | -60 |
  | iPhone 17 | 1206x2622 | 1206/1206 | 349x365pt | +15 | 0 |
  | iPhone Air | 1260x2736 | 1260/1260 | 366x382pt | +13 | +6 |
  | iPhone 17 Pro | 1206x2622 | 1206/1206 | 349x365pt | +15 | 0 |
  | iPhone 17 Pro Max | 1320x2868 | 1320/1320 | 378x394pt | +23 | -13 |

  The offsets are the widget against the app, found by correlating a patch of
  the app's render into the Home Screen shot - mean difference 0.3 to 0.4, so
  the match is the same static tile rather than something that merely looks
  similar. **They are not a layout fault, and they are not a clean measurement
  either**: see the note under "Not checked".
- **The app is 946MB and the .ipa 933MB.** It was 262MB when one starter was
  bundled; three are bundled now, at roughly 100MB of fonts and frames each.
  Under Apple's ceiling, but a heavy download, and bundling fewer starters is
  the only lever that moves it.
- **First run works on a clean device.** No empty state, no permission wall, no
  crash - the bundled starter design draws immediately.
- **The widget draws on real hardware.** `OK PLACED bundled/UIAppFonts
  anim=true` at 10MB against a 45MB ceiling, 32 of 32 lanes registered and
  resolvable, no font failures, no decode errors, 16fps over an 80-frame loop.
  The phone reports `family=4`, `systemExtraLargePortrait` - the family the
  designs are cut for, which no simulator here offers.
- **676 unit tests and 2 UI tests pass**, including against the new app group.
- **Launching other apps needs no `LSApplicationQueriesSchemes`.** The router
  calls `UIApplication.open` directly and never `canOpenURL`.
- **`UILaunchScreen` present, orientation locked to portrait, app group
  entitlements consistent across both targets.**
- **The support page and privacy policy are live** at
  `motionary-app.vercel.app` and `/privacy`, both returning 200, and the
  About screen links to the same two pages.

## Not checked

- Behaviour on a physical device other than the iPhone 17 Pro. The derived
  geometry has been exercised on simulators only; the note on `DeviceModel`
  records that an earlier derivation was 22px out on real hardware, and only
  hardware settles it.
- **Widget alignment cannot be settled on a simulator at all.** Designs are cut
  for the portrait extra-large family; every simulator here falls back to
  `systemLarge` (`family=2` on all five) where the phone reports `family=4`. So
  the widget is told it sits at the extra-large's origin while the system
  places a systemLarge somewhere else, and the offsets in the table above are
  that difference. The horizontal half is explained exactly by it - a
  systemLarge is centred, so the predicted offset is
  `(screen - handedOver*3)/2` minus the derived origin, which lands within
  1.5px of every measurement:

  | device | assumed x | systemLarge at | predicted | measured |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | iPhone 17e | 62.1 | 72.0 | +9.9 | +10 |
  | iPhone 17 / 17 Pro | 64.0 | 79.5 | +15.5 | +15 |
  | iPhone Air | 66.9 | 81.0 | +14.1 | +13 |
  | iPhone 17 Pro Max | 70.0 | 93.0 | +23.0 | +23 |

  So these numbers say nothing about the error on a real phone, where the
  family is the one the design was cut for. What they do say is that the
  vertical origin is the weak part of the derivation: it lands dead on for the
  17 Pro, which is the measured phone, and is 60px out on the 17e. That is the
  same warning `DeviceModel` already carries, now with a second data point.
  Alignment on any other phone needs that phone.
- What App Review makes of the brand icons. Server-side validation passed, but
  that checks the package, not the guidelines.
- VoiceOver, Dynamic Type and RTL layout.
- The widget under system memory pressure, as opposed to at rest.
